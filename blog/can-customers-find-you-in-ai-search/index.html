84% of US small business owners are confident customers can find them online. 46% haven't checked AI search.

Here is a pair of numbers that does not sit comfortably together. In BrightLocal's Small Business Marketing Report 2026, 84% of US small business owners say they are confident potential customers can easily find them online. Yet 46% have never checked whether their business appears when a customer asks an AI tool for a recommendation, or do not know whether it appears. And AI search is no longer a side channel: 45% of consumers used AI tools for local business recommendations in the last year, up from just 6% the year before. [1][2]

The study

This is the third chapter of BrightLocal's Small Business Marketing Report 2026, published October 21, 2026. It draws on a representative panel of 1,002 US owners of locally operating businesses with 1 to 50 employees, all of them responsible for marketing decisions in their business. BrightLocal pairs the survey with its own consumer research and with an analysis of 200,000 real AI search prompts across 1,300 business locations. [1][2][3]

What owners believe vs what they keep up

Owners are not ignorant of AI search. 86% know consumers use AI tools to find and choose local businesses, and 67% say they understand well how that works. 54% have checked and are confident their business appears in AI results, while a further 21% believe it probably appears but have not checked - which is how the report gets to 46% who have not checked or do not know. [1]

65% of owners say they are actively working on their AI visibility. That is more than the share keeping their Google Business Profile active (35%, though 85% have one), keeping their website active (41%), or staying active on other review sites and directories (26%). The catch is that AI tools draw heavily on exactly those sources: in BrightLocal's citation analysis, Google Business Profile, Yelp, Facebook, Tripadvisor and OpenTable were the most frequently cited directories in local business searches. A business that is quiet in those places is asking AI tools to recommend it on stale information. [1][3]

How customers actually use AI search

BrightLocal's consumer research shows how AI fits into a real search. 23% of consumers used AI during their most recent search for a local business, but only 2% used it as their only channel - 43% went on to Google and 39% to social media for more information. Just 8% choose AI as their first channel. AI is where people shortlist; Google and social are where they verify. And decisions are fast: most consumers (75%) find a local business in under 30 minutes, and 72% look at three or fewer businesses before deciding. Being in the AI shortlist matters, and so does being findable and current wherever the customer checks next. [1][2]

Why searching your own name proves nothing

When BrightLocal analyzed 200,000 non-branded prompts across 1,300 business locations, the businesses that AI tools recommended were not consistent even for the same prompt on the same tool. People also do not prompt the way they used to search: instead of "dentist in Brooklyn", they write long, specific requests like "need an emergency dentist open late in Brooklyn tonight". Finding your business when you search its name is encouraging, but it tells you nothing about whether you appear when a stranger describes the service they need. [3]

Fair warnings

  • BrightLocal sells local SEO and AI-visibility tools, including a paid tracker for exactly the problem this report describes. The numbers can be accurate and the framing still serve the seller. Treat the urgency, not just the data, with that in mind. [1]
  • The owner figures are self-reported: confidence, understanding and "actively working on AI visibility" are what owners say about themselves, not measured behavior. Owners who answer marketing surveys may also be more marketing-aware than average. [1]
  • AI visibility is unstable by nature. BrightLocal's own 200,000-prompt analysis found the same question can surface different businesses on different runs, so no tactic can guarantee you appear - and anyone selling guaranteed AI rankings is overpromising. [3]

What to do with this

What follows is our read - judgment, not the study's prescription. The survey and the consumer data are American; treat the exact percentages as directional, not as measurements of your own market.

  1. Check realistic prompts, not your name. Write down five things a stranger would actually type - "24 hour electrician in Indiranagar", not your business name - and run them in two or three AI tools. Note whether you appear, which competitors do, and which sources the tools seem to rely on.
  2. Feed the sources AI reads. Google Business Profile, Yelp, Facebook, Tripadvisor and OpenTable were the most-cited directories in BrightLocal's analysis. Keep hours, services, photos and reviews current there before spending on anything labeled "AI optimization". [3]
  3. Treat it as upkeep, not a project. BrightLocal's CEO Myles Anderson puts it plainly: AI rewards fresh information that stays consistent across sources, and "this isn't one-and-done work. A strong month followed by three quiet ones won't hold your visibility." A monthly fifteen minutes beats an annual overhaul. [1]

Try it this week

Search for your service the way a stranger would - the problem, the place, the urgency - in ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's AI mode. Note whether you appear and who does. Then repeat it next month. And if you do only one thing afterwards, log into your Google Business Profile and bring it up to date.
